The problem

Create a reference that stays useful when its environment changes.

A voltage reference sits beneath many mixed-signal blocks, so its quality affects the whole system. This project explored a resistorless 65 nm bandgap topology, combining temperature-dependent device voltages while controlling bias, startup, power, and sensitivity to supply variation.

The goal was never simply a good voltage at room temperature. The reference had to be assessed across temperature, supply and startup conditions, then checked for low-frequency noise and variation.

Temperature & supply behavior

A useful reference requires more than a nominal operating point.

Temperature sweep

Reported VREF range across −40°C to +125°C gives a calculated temperature coefficient of approximately 30.8 ppm/°C.

Supply rejection

Reported PSRR is 34.98 dB at 10 Hz. The curve was also useful for understanding the loop's frequency-dependent behavior.

Startup verification

Fast startup, with a slow tail to final settling.

The start-up simulation reports approximately 3.27 µs to reach 90% of the final output. The later tail toward the final value is a reminder that the selected settling criterion matters: a circuit can be ready for use before every internal node is fully quiet.

Failure analysis

The noise miss revealed the design trade-off.

The 0.1–10 Hz output-noise result was approximately 122.6 µVpp, above the 50 µVpp target. The report's noise-contribution summary points to PMOS current-source devices as the main flicker-noise contributors. Increasing their area can reduce flicker noise, but adds parasitic capacitance and can slow settling. That makes the central Rev. B trade-off explicit: noise ↔ startup time ↔ power.

Line regulation

1.60% / V

The other miss was line regulation. It cannot be inferred directly from PSRR: the two metrics probe different supply-to-output mechanisms. The next iteration should revisit DC supply sensitivity and bias-loop headroom.

Rev. B

What I would change before layout.

Rebalance flicker noise

Resize and re-bias the PMOS current-source devices, then recheck integrated noise against the startup penalty created by added capacitance.

Strengthen DC supply behavior

Investigate the supply-sensitive bias paths and operating headroom to reduce line regulation without assuming a good PSRR result is sufficient.

Finish the implementation flow

Complete layout, DRC/LVS, parasitic extraction, and post-layout PVT and Monte Carlo verification before making any tapeout-quality claim.
